AI on the food line

How our research turns a production line into one that learns, in four steps from measurement to action.

  1. 01Sensing

    Sensors and cameras measure the temperature, color, texture and composition of foods and processes in real time.

  2. 02Modeling

    Physics-based models and digital twins reproduce heat and mass transfer inside foods, including regions no sensor can reach. Response surface methodology (RSM) combined with AI maps how process variables shape the final product.

  3. 03Prediction

    ML and DL models forecast quality, safety and shelf life from sensor and simulation data.

  4. 04Application

    Predictions guide real decisions: optimizing processing conditions, sorting raw materials and designing safer, longer-lasting foods.

Join FMEL

Curious minds are always welcome

FMEL welcomes M.S. and Ph.D. students and undergraduate research interns who are curious about food and the technology behind it. No background in AI or programming is needed. If the topic interests you, you can learn the tools here, together with the lab.

Students from food science, chemical and mechanical engineering, computer science, statistics and many other fields are all welcome. Some of the things you could work on:

  • Digital twins of thermal processing
  • AI models for food quality, spoilage and shelf life
  • Smarter, data-guided processing and upcycling of food by-products
  • Smart packaging and food-safety sensing

Feel free to email Prof. Hansol Doh just to say hello or ask a question. Prospective graduate students are encouraged to start with an undergraduate research internship.
